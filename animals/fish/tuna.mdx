title: "Tuna"
description: "A guide to tuna, fast, warm-bodied ocean fish built for long-distance swimming."
---

Tuna are bony fish in the tribe Thunnini, part of the mackerel family (Scombridae). They live in open ocean waters worldwide and are among the fastest and most powerful fish in the sea. Well-known species include bluefin, yellowfin, albacore, bigeye, and skipjack.

## Built for speed

Tuna have a streamlined, torpedo-shaped body suited to fast, efficient swimming.

- **Crescent-shaped tail** provides powerful thrust with little drag.
- **Retractable fins** fold into grooves on the body to reduce resistance.
- **Constant swimming** pushes water over their gills. Many tuna must keep moving to breathe.

## Warm-bodied fish

Unlike most fish, tuna can keep parts of their body warmer than the surrounding water. A heat-exchange system of blood vessels, called a *rete mirabile*, retains heat produced by their muscles. This keeps their muscles, eyes, and brain working well in cold, deep water.

<Tip>
  Bluefin tuna are especially good at this, which lets them hunt in cold waters from the tropics to near the Arctic.
</Tip>

## Size and migration

The Atlantic bluefin is the largest tuna. It can reach over 3 meters (10 feet) and weigh more than 600 kg (1,300 pounds). Many tuna species migrate thousands of kilometers across ocean basins between feeding and spawning grounds.

## Diet

Tuna are predators that hunt in schools, feeding on smaller fish, squid, and crustaceans.

<Warning>
  Tuna are heavily fished worldwide. Some populations, especially of bluefin, have declined sharply due to overfishing.
</Warning>
