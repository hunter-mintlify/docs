Salmon are bony fish in the family Salmonidae, native to the North Atlantic and North Pacific. Most species are anadromous: they hatch in fresh water, migrate to the ocean to grow, and return to fresh water to reproduce.

## Life cycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Egg">
    Females lay eggs in gravel nests called redds in cold, clear streams.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fry and smolt">
    Young salmon spend months to years in fresh water, then undergo physical changes that prepare them for salt water.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ocean adult">
    Adults feed and grow at sea for one to several years.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spawning run">
    Adults swim upstream, often leaping waterfalls, to spawn in the stream where they hatched.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Navigation

Salmon find their natal streams largely by smell, recognizing the chemical signature of their home waters. Scientists also believe they use Earth's magnetic field to navigate across the open ocean.

## Species

Pacific species include chinook (king), coho, sockeye, pink, and chum. The Atlantic salmon is the single species native to the Atlantic Ocean.

<Note>
  Most Pacific salmon die after spawning once. Atlantic salmon can survive and spawn multiple times.
</Note>
