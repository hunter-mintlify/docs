Fish are cold-blooded aquatic vertebrates that breathe through gills and typically swim using fins. With more than 35,000 known species, they make up roughly half of all living vertebrate species.

## Main groups

Fish are commonly divided into three broad groups:

| Group | Skeleton | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jawless fish | Cartilage | Lampreys, hagfish |
| Cartilaginous fish | Cartilage | Sharks, rays, chimaeras |
| Bony fish | Bone | Salmon, clownfish, tuna |

## Key traits

- **Gills** extract dissolved oxygen from water as it flows over thin filaments.
- **Fins** provide propulsion, steering, and stability.
- **Lateral line** is a sensory organ that detects vibrations and water movement.
- **Swim bladder** (in most bony fish) is a gas-filled organ that controls buoyancy.

<Tip>
  Sharks have no swim bladder. They rely on a large, oil-rich liver and constant swimming to avoid sinking.
</Tip>
