Clownfish, also called anemonefish, are small, brightly colored fish found in warm waters of the Indian and Pacific Oceans. There are about 30 recognized species, most with orange, black, or white markings.

## Life with anemones

Clownfish live among the stinging tentacles of sea anemones. A protective mucus layer on their skin keeps them from being stung. The relationship benefits both animals:

- The anemone shelters the clownfish from predators.
- The clownfish chases away fish that eat anemones and provides nutrients through its waste.

## Social structure

Each anemone hosts a group with a strict size-based hierarchy. The largest fish is the breeding female, the second largest is the breeding male, and the rest are smaller non-breeding males.

<Tip>
  All clownfish are born male. If the female dies, the dominant male changes sex to become the new female.
</Tip>

## Diet

Clownfish are omnivores. They eat algae, zooplankton, and small invertebrates, along with leftover food from their host anemone.
