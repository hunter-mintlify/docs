title: "Seahorses"
description: "A guide to seahorses, upright-swimming fish where the males carry the young."

Seahorses are small bony fish in the genus *Hippocampus*, named for their horse-like heads. They live in shallow tropical and temperate waters around the world, usually among seagrass beds, coral reefs, and mangroves. There are around 46 recognized species.

## Body and movement

Seahorses swim upright, which makes them unusual among fish. Instead of scales, their bodies are covered in bony plates under thin skin.

- **Dorsal fin** on the back beats rapidly to push them forward.
- **Pectoral fins** near the head help with steering.
- **Prehensile tail** grips seagrass and coral so they can anchor in place.

Seahorses are among the slowest-swimming fish, so they rely on camouflage rather than speed. Many species can change color to match their surroundings.

## Male pregnancy

Seahorses are one of the few animals where the male carries the young.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Courtship">
    Pairs often perform daily greeting dances, swimming side by side and changing color.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Egg transfer">
    The female deposits her eggs into a brood pouch on the male's belly, where he fertilizes them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pregnancy">
    The male carries the developing embryos in his pouch for roughly two to four weeks, depending on the species.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Birth">
    The male releases fully formed miniature seahorses, sometimes hundreds or more at once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Diet

Seahorses are ambush predators that feed on small crustaceans such as tiny shrimp. They have no stomach, so food passes through quickly and they need to eat often.

<Warning>
  Many seahorse species are threatened by habitat loss, bycatch, and collection for the aquarium and traditional medicine trades.
</Warning>
